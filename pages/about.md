# Values

## How I Learn and Build

### Verify, Don’t Inherit
Popular doesn’t mean proven. When a system breaks or an API misbehaves, I strip it down to primitives I can verify myself instead of trusting a framework’s black box or an inherited rulebook.

### Build to Learn
Diagrams and mental models aren’t the work. I learn most when code exists and reality can push back. A rough prototype teaches me more, faster, than a flawless design on paper.

### Define “Done” First
Effort aimed at a vague goal is expensive noise. Before I open an editor or write a prompt, I write down what “done” means in measurable terms. Then I move fast.

### Keep the Loop Closed
Knowledge decays when it goes unchallenged. I re-test my assumptions about models, pipelines, and tools as they change, and I write here partly to expose the gaps in what I think I know.

### Own the Failure
When something breaks, I own the fix. Stack traces, failing tests, and edge cases are telemetry, and I use them to harden the design and raise my own standards.
